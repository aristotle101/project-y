# Rewrite Playbook

Detailed guidance loaded on demand by the `humanize-writing` skill. Read this when a rewrite needs more than the base rules in `SKILL.md`.

## Rewrite Sequence

1. **Read for intent.** Find the one thing the draft is actually trying to say. Everything else supports it.
2. **Cut to the spine.** Delete filler, hedges, and repeated framing before touching wording.
3. **Reorder.** Lead with the point a person would say first out loud, not the machine's symmetrical intro-body-conclusion.
4. **Rewrite line by line.** Vary sentence length. Break long clauses. Let some sentences be short.
5. **Restore concrete detail.** Swap abstract nouns for the real thing being described.
6. **Read aloud (mentally).** If a line has no rhythm a person would use, rewrite it.
7. **Final pass for register.** Confirm casual stayed casual, formal stayed formal.

## Stronger Rules

- One idea per sentence when the idea is heavy. Combine only when the ideas belong together.
- Kill "It's worth noting," "In today's world," "When it comes to," "At the end of the day," and similar throat-clearing.
- Cut adverb + weak verb pairs ("effectively manages") in favor of a strong verb ("runs").
- Prefer the active voice unless the actor genuinely doesn't matter.
- Don't open three sentences in a row the same way.
- Use contractions in casual and most business writing; avoid them only in formal or legal registers.
- Numbers, names, quotes, and links are frozen — never paraphrase them.
- Don't add a summary sentence that repeats what was just said.

## AI Anti-Patterns to Delete

- Tricolon overuse: "fast, reliable, and scalable" on every line.
- "Not only… but also" scaffolding.
- Empty transitions: "Furthermore," "Moreover," "Additionally" stacked back to back.
- Symmetrical bullets that all start with the same verb form when the content isn't parallel.
- Over-hedging: "may potentially," "could possibly help."
- Grandiose closers: "In conclusion, this represents a significant step forward."
- Em-dash pairs used as a default rhythm crutch on every sentence.

## Before / After Examples

**Before (AI smell 4):**
> In today's fast-paced digital landscape, leveraging cutting-edge solutions is essential for businesses seeking to optimize their workflows and drive meaningful results.

**After:**
> Teams move faster when their tools get out of the way. That's the whole pitch.

---

**Before (AI smell 3):**
> I wanted to reach out to kindly follow up regarding the status of the project, as I believe it would be beneficial to align on next steps at your earliest convenience.

**After:**
> Quick follow-up — where are we on the project? Happy to jump on a call to sort out next steps.

## Language-Specific Habits

### English
- Short sentences carry weight. Don't be afraid of a two-word line.
- Contractions signal a human. Use them.
- Idioms are fine when they're natural, not decorative.

### Thai (ไทย)
- ภาษาพูดกับภาษาเขียนต่างกันมาก — เลือกให้ตรงกับช่องทาง (แชท/อีเมล/โพสต์).
- คำลงท้าย (ครับ/ค่ะ/นะ/เลย) ช่วยให้เป็นธรรมชาติ แต่อย่าใส่จนเฝือ.
- หลีกเลี่ยงการแปลตรงตัวจากอังกฤษ เช่น "ในโลกที่เปลี่ยนแปลงอย่างรวดเร็ว" — คนไทยไม่พูดแบบนี้.
- ประโยคไทยไม่ต้องมีประธานทุกครั้ง ตัดออกได้ถ้าเข้าใจจากบริบท.
- ผสมไทย-อังกฤษได้ถ้าเป็นคำที่คนใช้จริง (เช่น meeting, deadline) อย่าฝืนแปลเป็นไทยทั้งหมด.

### General multilingual
- Keep code-switching only where a native speaker would actually switch.
- Localize punctuation (quotation marks, spacing, list style) to the target language.
- Translate meaning and tone, not word order.

## When to Ask vs. Assume

- Ambiguous target language → keep the source's dominant language, state the assumption in one line.
- Ambiguous register → match the draft's existing register; don't formalize casual text.
- Ambiguous audience → assume the most likely reader for the channel and note it briefly.
